# Answer a desktop card in the composer

## How do I answer a desktop review or clarification in the composer?

The main composer answers the front card when that card accepts words and the draft is empty. Its quiet chip says `Replying to` followed by the card title. The placeholder gives the question or `Tell it what to change`. Enter or Command+Enter sends the answer to that card. Shift+Enter still adds a line. For a review, those words are the `Not right` reason. Accept, Allow, and choice buttons stay on the card; typing does not silently choose one. There is no separate Tell it button or text field inside the card.

An answer clears only after the engine accepts it. A refused answer keeps the draft and says `That did not go through. Try again.`

## Why did my existing desktop draft not become an answer?

A draft written before the card arrived stays a chat draft. The chip offers `Reply to` followed by the title. Click that offer to bind the draft to the card. Until then, Enter sends an ordinary message.

## How do I dismiss Replying to and send an ordinary desktop message?

Click the chip's x (`Dismiss reply`), or press Escape in an empty bound composer. This removes the binding. The next Enter sends a chat message. Escape in a composer containing text keeps the reply binding and blurs the field.

## Does paging the desktop tray change which card my composer answers?

Paging brings another card to the front. A bound composer follows the front card when it accepts words. Later folds the current card away and brings the next one forward. Later on the last card removes the binding. A card that accepts only buttons has no text binding. An Other option binds the composer for your own answer.

## How do I say why I denied a desktop permission?

The permission buttons remain explicit: typing into an ordinary composer never allows or denies a command. Click `and say why` to bind the main composer to the optional Deny reason. Enter sends Deny with those words through the answer endpoint. There is no second text field in the card. The chip's x restores ordinary chat.
