# Desktop place navigation

## How do I go to a place and restore its tabs in the desktop app?

Click a place in the left rail, or choose it in Go to (Command+P) and press Enter. The place's Home opens as a tab in the strip you already have, right after the active tab, and takes focus; no tab is replaced (see desktop-place-libraries). A click on a place inside a page (a tile on All places, a breadcrumb) does the same. Going to the same place again focuses its Home tab instead of opening another. Visiting puts the place in the rail's Open list, newest first. Clicking Now shows every open tab and ends any Focus. All places opens as a Home tab in the current strip.

Going to a place never stops running work or touches your tabs, groups and drafts. Unknown and archived destinations cannot be opened; restore an archived place from All places first.

## How do I close a desktop place and reopen its saved tabs?

Use the rail row's Close action or the close-place shortcut. Closing a place takes it off the rail and ends a Focus on it; it closes no tab. Go to the place again to put it back on the rail. The place's chats, Home and history remain intact. A closed place with running work or a question stays muted in Open until that work settles.

Close all others closes the other unpinned Open places. Pinned places remain. Now and All places cannot be closed as places. If the engine refuses a close, the place stays open and the error is shown. Bulk close stops at the first refusal; earlier accepted closes remain applied.

## How do I go up a level from a desktop place Home?

On Home, press Command+Up on macOS or Control+Up elsewhere. It opens the primary parent, the first parent in the place graph. A top-level place opens All places as a Home tab in the current workspace.

## How do I open a place in a new window?

Choose Open in new window from a place's menu. In the desktop app, the new window opens on that place and the current window keeps its place and tabs. In a browser build, the action goes to the place in the same window.
