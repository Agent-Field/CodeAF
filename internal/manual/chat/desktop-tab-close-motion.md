# Desktop tab close motion

## What happens when I close a desktop tab?

Closing a desktop tab takes it out of the strip. The tab stays on screen for a moment while its width shrinks to nothing, and the tabs beside it slide into the gap. That takes the same short time as other ordinary motion in the app (a fifth of a second). The closing tab cannot be clicked, and a screen reader does not keep announcing it while it shrinks. The conversation it showed keeps running; closing the tab does not stop the work.

## Do the other desktop tabs slide when one closes?

Yes. The closing tab gives up its width, and the tabs on either side move across to fill that space. They end up with the same small gap they have between any other two tabs. A tab that is in a group shrinks inside that group. Opening a tab does not slide: a new desktop tab fades in where it will sit, and it does not grow or shrink as it appears.

## Why does a new desktop tab fade in?

A new desktop tab fades in where it will sit. It does not scale, grow, or shrink as it appears, so the place you click stays put. With reduced motion it appears at once, with no fade.

## How do I stop the desktop tab close animation?

Turn on reduced motion in the system settings. The closing tab then disappears at once, and the tabs beside it are already in their new places. A new tab appears at once too, with no fade. There is no separate switch inside codeaf; reduced motion follows the system.
