# Undoing a tab change in the desktop app

## Undo a closed desktop tab — put it back where it was, in its group

Closing a tab, the other tabs, the tabs to the right, or a whole group can be taken back. The tab comes back at the place it stood, in the same group, and a group that closing had emptied comes back with its name. The same undo puts back a move, a pin, a new group, taking a tab out of a group, and merging or separating a split. Command Z on a Mac, and Ctrl Z on Linux, takes back the last of those. One window remembers twenty. The twenty-first drop forgets the oldest. There is no redo.

## How many structural undos does one desktop window keep

Each window keeps its own twenty. Undoing in one window does not take back a change made in another. Closing the window forgets that window's steps. Opening it again starts with none. A draft, a title, a view and which tab is selected are not steps.

## Draft typing is not undoable in the desktop tab strip

Typing in a tab, renaming it, or moving around inside it is not undone. Undo of a close leaves those words as they are now. With nothing to undo, the key does nothing.

## Closing toast Undo for a desktop tab I just closed

The closing toast's Undo puts the closed tab, or the closed group, back where it stood, the same as undoing that close. It does not take back what you typed afterwards.
