# Collapsed rail places

## How do the arrow keys work in the desktop place switcher

With the side rail hidden the strip has no place switcher: the Home tab is an ordinary tab, not a menu. Press Command+P (Control+P on Linux) for Go to, which lists Now, then pinned places, then open places, then All places. There is no Inbox row. Up and Down move through those rows, Enter opens the place's Home as a tab, and Escape closes the list.

## What does control 2 do from the home tab

Places are numbered. On a Mac, Control 0 is Now and Control 1 through Control 9 are the pinned places and then the open ones. Elsewhere those chords are Alt 0 through Alt 9, because Control and a digit already jumps tabs. Control 2, or Alt 2, opens the Home of whichever place is in that slot as a tab, or focuses it if it is open. All places is Command Shift P on a Mac and Ctrl Shift P elsewhere.

## Which place needs you when the rail is hidden

The strip no longer says which other place needs you when the rail is hidden. The frame pill counts the questions waiting in other conversations and Next up walks them. Show the rail to see the amber dot on the place's row: a failed task is a red dot, and running work draws no dot.

## Why is one place row filled

In the rail, the filled row is the place of the tab in front. The fill is the soft field colour. A place that lives under another shows that parent's name afterwards, in the quieter colour. Pinned places are listed before open ones.
