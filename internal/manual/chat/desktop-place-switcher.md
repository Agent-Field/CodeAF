# Collapsed rail places

## How do the arrow keys work in the desktop place switcher

Hide the side rail and click the home tab. A menu opens under that tab. It lists Now, then pinned places, then open places, then All places. There is no Inbox row. Up and Down move through those rows. Enter goes to the row you are on. Escape closes the menu and puts the keyboard back on the home tab.

## What does control 2 do from the home tab

Places are numbered. On a Mac, Control 0 is Now and Control 1 through Control 9 are the pinned places and then the open ones. Elsewhere those chords are Alt 0 through Alt 9, because Control and a digit already jumps tabs. Control 2, or Alt 2, jumps to whichever place is in that slot, including while the menu under the home tab is open. The row shows that chord. All places is Command Shift P on a Mac and Ctrl Shift P elsewhere.

## Which place needs you when the rail is hidden

When another place needs you and the rail is hidden, the home tab's colour square picks up an amber dot and the tab says which place: "1 needs you in <place>", or "need" when there is more than one. The menu under the home tab draws a six-pixel amber dot on that place's row and uses the same words. A failed task is a red dot and names the place. Running work draws no dot. The words stay the ordinary colour.

## Why is one place row filled

The place you are in is the filled row in the menu under the home tab. The fill is the soft field colour. A place that lives under another shows that parent's name afterwards, in the quieter colour. Pinned places are listed before open ones.
