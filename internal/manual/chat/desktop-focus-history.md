# Desktop focus history

## Does the desktop remember the tabs and places I visited?

The desktop window keeps a focus history. Three moves are steps: changing tabs, switching places, and drilling into a task inside a tab. Opening a tab in the background does not count, because that tab never took focus. Putting the window's saved tabs back on launch is not a new step either.

There is no permanent Back button and no permanent Forward button.

## How do I go back to the tab, place, scroll and draft I left?

On a Mac, Command [ goes back and Command ] goes forward. On Linux and Windows, Alt Left and Alt Right do. Each step puts back the place, the tab, where that conversation was scrolled, and the draft that tab was holding. A task you had opened in that tab comes back with the step. Going back does not add another step.

## When does the back chip show, and when does it go away?

After a jump you did not start yourself, such as Next up, a chip at the left of the tab strip reads "Back to" and the name of where you were. Your next click or key, other than the chip itself, folds the chip. Pressing the chip goes back to that place and tab.
