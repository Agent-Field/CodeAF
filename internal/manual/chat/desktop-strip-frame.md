# Tab strip frame

## Where the frame pill sits on the desktop tab strip

The frame pill sits at the right end of the tab strip, after the tabs and after the empty space that drags the window. It is not inside a tab. It reads how many questions need you in other conversations, for example "1 need you elsewhere", and the key: ⌘J, or Ctrl J on Windows and Linux. Hovering it opens the list of those questions. A click, or that key, starts Next up at the front of the list. A click on a row starts Next up at that question. The pill is absent when the count is zero. It never counts the conversation you are in.

## The banner under the frame pill

A blocking question that arrives in another conversation slides a banner out from under the pill. The first line is the ask. The second line names the conversation and, when the feed says so, what it is holding up, for example "Config parser · holding up 2 tasks". It stays about 4 seconds, then folds back into the pill. Questions that were already waiting when the window opened stay on the pill and do not raise a banner. Switching away from a conversation does not turn a question that was already in the feed into a banner.

## Where the back chip is on the tab strip

After a jump you did not start, a chip at the left of the tab strip reads "Back to" the conversation you left. It sits before the Home tab. When the rail is collapsed, the rail toggle stays at the left edge and the chip sits after it, still before Home. Your next action, including a key, folds the chip. Pressing it selects that tab again. Scroll and the draft stay with the tab. A task you had drilled into is not reopened by the chip.

## Focus mode and the collapsed rail

Focus mode hides the tab strip, and the frame pill with it. Moving the pointer to the top edge of the window brings the strip back, and the pill is on it. Collapsing the rail leaves the strip in place, so the pill stays visible.
