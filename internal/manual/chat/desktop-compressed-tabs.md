# Desktop compressed tabs

## Why are the desktop tabs only icons on a narrow window?

When the desktop window is 600px wide or narrower, a tab you are not in becomes a 44px chip. The chip shows that tab's icon, or a small dot when the tab needs you or has failed. The name is not written on the chip. The tab you are in keeps its name and stays at least 112px wide. A pinned tab stays the smaller icon it already is, and a place's Home tab keeps its name. Making the window wider than 600px puts the names back.

## How do I read a compressed tab's full title in the desktop app?

Hover the icon, or move to it with the keyboard. A tooltip shows the full title. The tab's accessible name is that title even while the chip shows only the icon. An inactive compressed tab does not open a preview card. A split you are not in is one chip; the tooltip names every pane in that split. Selecting it shows the panes again.

## Where do desktop tabs that do not fit on a narrow strip go?

The strip still scrolls, and the +N control lists every open tab, including tabs that are scrolled out of sight. Choosing one there selects it. The new-tab and all-tabs controls stay on the strip.
