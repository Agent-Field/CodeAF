# Desktop decision log, status line and overturn routes

## The decision log — see everything codeaf decided for me automatically, newest first

Each place keeps a log of what codeaf decided on your behalf: what it did, which place's rule decided it, why, how sure it was, and whether you later overturned it. The desktop reads it page by page, newest first, 50 rows at a time and never more than 200 in one read. A place that has decided nothing has an empty log, and Home draws nothing for it. A decision you overturn stays in the log, marked with the time you reversed it.

## The Home status line — Deciding automatically, Learning, Always ask

The line under a place's name is built from that place's own log. “Deciding automatically” means at least one kind of question has earned the right to be decided without asking. “Learning” means codeaf is still watching what you pick. “Always ask” shows when the place, or the nearest place above it that states a choice, is set to always ask. A place that has never been asked anything has no line at all. The count (“41 of 44 agreed this week”) covers the last seven days: decisions you did not overturn, out of all decisions made. Zero is never drawn.

## Overturn a decision — what happens and when it refuses

Overturning reverses only the one decision, and you say what happens next time: “ask” makes that kind of question ask you again, “keep” sends it back to learning. Overturning the same decision twice does not undo it twice. The answer lists the tasks and decisions that used it and offers to notify or pause them; nothing is changed without your separate choice. If the decision cannot be reversed any more, the answer is “This decision cannot be undone.” and nothing changes. If this engine cannot reverse decisions at all, the route says “not implemented” instead of pretending.

## The sureness setting — how sure before it decides, and Always ask

Each place can state its own sureness threshold (1 to 100 percent, 90 by default) and whether to always ask. Clearing a place's own choice makes it follow the nearest place above it again. A threshold outside 1–100 is refused with a sentence, and saving the value already in force changes nothing.
