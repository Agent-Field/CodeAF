# Desktop place clicks open tabs

## What happens when I click a place tile on All places or in a breadcrumb in the desktop app?

Clicking a place anywhere inside a page opens that place's Home as a new tab in the window you are in, immediately after the active tab, and focuses it. Your tabs, splits and groups are not replaced and the window keeps its tint. If that place's Home is already open as a tab here, that tab is focused instead of a second one opening. This applies to place tiles on All places and on a place's Home, search results, the breadcrumb, a Quick Look's Go to button and a "Home" link. Opening a chat from a page works the same way: it opens as a tab, or focuses the tab already showing it.

## How do I open a place in the background without leaving my current tab?

Command-click on a Mac, Control-click on anything else, or middle-click a tile or a breadcrumb name. The place's Home opens as a tab behind the active one and focus stays where it is. Open in new window is still in the place's context menu and on Command+Enter (Control+Enter) over a tile.

## Does the rail or a page click switch the window to a place, and how do I get back to my tabs?

A rail click now does the same as a click inside a page: places are libraries, so it opens the place's Home as a tab or focuses the open one, and never replaces your tabs (see "Does clicking a place in the desktop rail replace my tabs?"). To see only one place's tabs, use Focus: the Focus button on a rail row, or Command/Control-click the row. The Now row shows every tab.

## How do I go back after switching places?

Command+[ (Control+[ on Linux with the shell shortcuts) steps Back across a place switch to the tab and place you left, and Command+] steps forward again.

## Limits

Archived or deleted places cannot be opened this way; the sentence says so and nothing changes. Next up and the Go to chooser open the place's Home as a tab, the same as the rail.
