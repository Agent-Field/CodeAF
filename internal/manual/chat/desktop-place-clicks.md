# Desktop place clicks open tabs

## What happens when I click a place tile on All places or in a breadcrumb in the desktop app?

Clicking a place anywhere inside a page opens that place's Home as a new tab in the window you are in, immediately after the active tab, and focuses it. Your tabs, splits and groups are not replaced and the window keeps its place and tint. If that place's Home is already open as a tab here, that tab is focused instead of a second one opening. This applies to place tiles on All places and on a place's Home, search results, the breadcrumb, a Quick Look's Go to button and a "Home" link. Opening a chat from a page works the same way: it opens as a tab, or focuses the tab already showing it.

## How do I open a place in the background without leaving my current tab?

Command-click on a Mac, Control-click on anything else, or middle-click a tile or a breadcrumb name. The place's Home opens as a tab behind the active one and focus stays where it is. Open in new window is still in the place's context menu and on Command+Enter (Control+Enter) over a tile.

## How do I move the window to a place and return to my earlier tabs?

Only the left rail changes the window's place: click a place row, or the Now row, or press Command+P (Command+Shift+P for All places) and Enter. The window then wears that place's tint and shows that place's own tabs. Leaving a place saves its exact tab set, so returning to Now brings back the tabs, their order, pinned tabs, groups, splits, which tab was active and where each page was scrolled, and a reload keeps them. Alt-click, or Command/Control-click, a rail row to open that place's Home as a tab instead of switching.

## How do I go back after switching places?

Command+[ (Control+[ on Linux with the shell shortcuts) steps Back across a place switch to the tab and place you left, and Command+] steps forward again.

## Limits

Archived or deleted places cannot be opened this way; the sentence says so and nothing changes. Next up and the Go to chooser still move the window to the place a conversation belongs to, because they are asked to take you there.
