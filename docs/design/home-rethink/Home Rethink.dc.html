Model we agreed on, before the pixels:
Home is a switcher, not a directory. Seconds between opens, 10–20 live chats, 3–5 projects — so the project tree buys five rows of scaffolding to reach twenty leaves. Flat ranked list, project demoted to a tag on the row, alt+g toggles grouping back.
needs you and moving stop being strips and become the sort order of the one list. Nothing empty draws a heading.
Seven places once spend earned one in turn 2 — visible tab bar, and every place is also reachable by typing its name — the typed surface offers places alongside chats, so the tab bar teaches and the keyboard is faster. A tab wears a count only when something in it changed, and alt+1…7 jumps straight to one.
Discoverability is solved by events, not inventories: since you left appears only when something happened on its own, and each line is a door into the page that owns it. You learn memory exists the day it tells you it learned something. An almost-empty page is the best teacher on the machine — it can spend the whole screen saying what it's for.
Growth → a page (tasks, standing, memory, search). One fact about the thing under the cursor → a band on the card. Crew, accounts, permissions, fixes, harnesses → settings, one page, checked monthly.

3the keyboard, properly — plus what the machine tidies behind your back
You caught a real bug, and it was mine. Turn 2 put a live composer at the bottom of every page and then asked for g, d, w, m, p, f. If every printable character goes to the composer — and it must, that's the product — then none of those keys can ever fire. Two of my chords were worse than unreachable: ctrl+m IS enter and ctrl+e IS end-of-line inside the very field homes/spend.go already binds it in. 3a is the law that fixes all of it, and its one clause worth stealing is: no key does anything that isn't drawn on screen right now — which makes the collision fix and the discoverability fix the same fix. 3b is the hero: hold a modifier and the whole map appears in cells you were already looking at.

2the design language, then the pages that need it — at real scale
A terminal has no font sizes, so hierarchy has to come from somewhere else. 2a is the scale I'd commit to — five levels built from brightness, case, indent, band and blank-line rhythm, in the omarchy spirit: high contrast, one accent, generous vertical air, nothing merely decorative. Everything after it is drawn on that scale. Scale is honest too: memory is 1,240 lines across 86 shelves, not six, because store/facts.go has nine kinds, four channels, a scope ladder and a retraction shelf, and a page that only works at six rows is a page that breaks the first real week.
2athe scale · five levels, no font sizes involved
1 · pagememory  bright, bold, one word, only in the tab bar

3 · subjectyou want terse answers  primary tier — the thing itself

5 · margin3h ago  tertiary, right-flushed, never load-bearing
on top of the five, three non-type devices
the bandwhere the cursor is  selection, and the subject goes bold inside it
the accent? waiting on you  amber, or ◐ cyan in flight — never both meanings at once
the gutter2ch page · 2ch glyph  one indent level, ever
rhythm
blank line = section break. rows never get one. a page is a stack of
tight blocks separated by air, which is what makes 40 rows scannable.
money
a figure under half a cent is stated in words — under a cent — because
tokens.Money rounds, and $0.0017 printed as $0.00 reads as free.
Four brightness tiers is all the palette has, so hierarchy that needs a fifth step borrows case, indent or air instead of inventing a colour. The under a cent rule is lifted from homes/standing.go's own comment, not invented.
