  - "`response.attempts` is NOT a number of requests any more. It is a MULTIPLIER on how long a call may go on trying — `lane.Role.GiveUp`, which is 90 seconds for a turn you are watching — so `3` means 270 seconds and never three identical sends. The default is 1, where it was 3, and a person who had written 3 into their profile when 3 was the default is now asking for three times the patience. The key and the environment variable (`AFORGE_RESPONSE_ATTEMPTS`) keep their names because the question a person is answering has not changed."
  - "taxonomy.Limits.TransportAttempts and taxonomy.DefaultTransportAttempts are DELETED. Limits.Patience (a float64, default 1.0) replaces them. Nothing in internal/taxonomy, internal/session or internal/provider bounds a number of attempts on the wire any more; the cut allowances (SilentCutAttempts 3, DegenerateCutAttempts 2, BlindCutAttempts 2) are the only counts left there and they count a SHAPE OF REPLY that came apart."
  - "taxonomy.Evidence.OutOfTime is how a caller says its own deadline is gone, and taxonomy's transportPolicy answers it with a hop or an ending through the same classifier. It is deliberately NOT Evidence.Spent: Spent says no SHAPE of this request could be served, which is a different claim and usually a false one, and the reason a person reads stays the shape that actually failed."
  - "lane.UsePatience is the ONE door the person's setting reaches a deadline through, and lane.Role.GiveUp multiplies by it. It is process-wide because a deadline is built in two places that cannot read a profile — lane.PlanFor and internal/provider's dispatcher — and internal/session publishes it where it resolves the profile's limits (taxonomy_boundary.go)."
  - "internal/provider/endpoints.go no longer walks the relaxation ladder. recoverFromRefusal is in dispatch.go and asks control.Next for each rung, records it as a control.MoveShape on the plan's move log, and stops at the plan's deadline. relaxationPlan stays where it is as the DATA — which fields a body carries is the encoder's fact. TestNoAttemptCountingLoopOutsideTheDispatcher now also refuses a range over relaxationPlan or a plan's Shapes outside dispatch.go."
  - "control.Plan.ShapeRefused is new and is what tells control.Next that a refusal was about the BYTES rather than about a machine. Without it a ladder asking for its rung would be handed a machine forever, because the serving set of such a request is usually OPEN and an open set never runs out."
  - "hedgeRace.tried is DELETED. A race claims a machine through control.Plan.Moves — the same log the dispatcher under every arm reads — and gives a claim back through the new MoveLog.Release when the purse refuses it. The head of the choice is written on that log when the race is built, so a rescue deciding in the same millisecond cannot be handed the machine the primary is on."
  - "maxArms (4) and ladderArms (1) are KEPT deliberately and are not a retry budget. They bound how many copies of one question may be in flight AT ONCE, which is money and concurrency (Dean & Barroso's tail-at-scale rescue), never how many times it may be asked again. The code says so, so nobody folds them later."
  - "session.errandTriesPerRung and session.titleAttempts are DELETED. An errand makes ONE request per rung and is bounded by roles.PatienceFor; the naming ladder asks again with a doubling wait for as long as titleWindow (two minutes) lasts. The turn loop's `for attempt < attempts` is a deadline, and the empty-200 re-ask ends at the turn's give-up."
  - "internal/session has the two recovery laws now (recovery_law_test.go): no loop may count attempt/attempts/tries/retry/retries in its header OR its post statement, and no constant may end in one of those words. The allowlists name five functions and seven constants, each with the line saying what it counts instead — a repair round is not a retry, because it sends the model's own malformed answer back, which is a different request."
  - "A failure that asks for NO wait still pays one after the first. An empty 200 and a mangled tool call are answered by rotation at once — internal/taxonomy's waitFor still returns no backoff for either, and the FIRST such move is still free — but from the second the wait doubles, because a ladder bounded by a deadline that pays nothing between two requests sends as fast as an endpoint can fail."
  - "The turn loop reads a failure TWICE when the wait in front of the next attempt would outlast the deadline, and writes ONE journal line, carrying the verdict it acted on. A ladder that paid its wait and came back to find the give-up gone used to give up silently with a chain the person configured unasked, which is #794's failure wearing a deadline."
  - "The manual's four counted promises are gone. `models-and-cost.md` no longer says a rate limit is waited out 'up to six attempts or two minutes' or that a turn 'spends its own four tries'; `tasks.md` no longer says a task's patience is '60 attempts or 10 minutes' and a turn's '6 attempts or 2 minutes'; `screen.md` and `sessions-and-rewind.md` no longer promise 'three naming attempts'. lanes.md gains `How long aforge keeps trying, and the one setting that changes it`, which states what response.attempts means now and what it will never buy."
  - "internal/session/loop.go has a clock seam (turnNow, turnBackoff) and title.go has its own wait seam (titleBackoff). They exist because every ladder in the package is bounded by a deadline now: a test that made waiting free would make the deadline unreachable rather than making the test fast, so a scenario moves the clock by the wait it was asked for — the same fiction internal/provider's `owed` keeps."

R3 (#858) folded eleven controllers into one deadline and named four things it
had deliberately not folded. This is those four, and the last of the counts with
them: the relaxation ladder asks the one move generator for its rung, a race
claims machines out of the plan every arm shares, and the only number a person
could ever turn about how hard aforge tries is read as what they meant by it —
more patience, which is more time, and never the same bytes sent to the same
machine again.
