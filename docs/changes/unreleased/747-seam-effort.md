The owner's question was whether the effort level should be clickable or
rotatable in model space, the way a task's is. It should, and the answer that
made it easy was to stop having two places: the model, how hard it is being
asked to think, and who served it are one sentence about the next turn, and the
tray chip was saying half of that six rows away from the other half — while the
seam said a different half of it again, as a colon nobody could press.

So the rung moved to where the model is, and the press means what a press on a
task's rung already means. The wheel is the chord and the click; the list of
five with what each one buys is `/effort`, because a gesture spent on rotating
cannot also open a list, and a capability whose only door is taken away has been
deleted by accident.

And the dial crosses the wire, which it never did. A hosted conversation had the
knob missing with nothing on the screen saying so — the worst of the three
possible states, and the one the absence law exists to end.

And then the cell did not appear at all on the owner's own install, on either
road, which is the same defect one turn deeper: the shipped setting is absence,
the cell was drawn from the resolved word, and absence drew nothing. A dial that
is invisible until you have already used it is not a dial — it is the reason the
tray chip was never found and this one was asked for. So absence has a name on
the seam now, `⠿ auto`, and a row of its own at the top of the ladder. The wheel
still cannot reach it: getting back is `/effort auto`, because handing the scope
back to whatever stands over it is a decision and not something a wheel should
do on its way past.
