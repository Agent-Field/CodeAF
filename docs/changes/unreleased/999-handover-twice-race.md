  - "\"A task never stays unowned past the end of a turn\" was read as the end of WHICHEVER turn ends next. It is the end of the turn that actually put the question in front of the model. How far one node's note has got is now a fact ON THE NODE (`TaskNode.handNote`, `decisionNote`): owed until a request carries it, read afterwards. It is on the node and not on a queue because the floor runs at the end of EVERY agent's turn and a worker reads its own children's decisions — a per-queue fact let a worker's turn spend a press made, and still waiting, somewhere else."
  - "`task.settle = auto` is the same question through another door and had the same defect: the landing is the model's by policy and the landing note is the whole of the asking, so a turn ending between the two spent it. A note drained at a step boundary whose turn is then cut — an interrupt, an oversize refusal, a checkpoint that ends the turn — has left the queue and reached nobody, and a mark at the drain would have let the floor spend a question no model read."
  - "A press that no turn will ever carry now comes back at once instead of waiting for the person's next turn. A turn that ends with nothing following it — interrupted, failed, or simply owing nobody a word — hands back what that agent owed (`Agent.handBackUnreadNotes`), because there is no longer a turn coming to ask. Until this, such a node stayed model-owned and home stopped counting it as something needing a person (`taskstatus.go`). `internal/manual/chat`'s `task-controls.md` and `tasks.md` said the old thing and now say which turn counts and what a stopped turn does."

---

Two doors, one law: nothing a turn never read is spent by that turn ending, and
nothing a closed conversation would say or write happens at all. The first is
assignment.go's own rule about a line said to a node, said about a decision
handed to the model; the second is one door (`Agent.writeIfOpen`) where three
readings each had their own idea of the question, and two of them had none.
