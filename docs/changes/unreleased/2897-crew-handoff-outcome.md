title: A crew manager is told what became of each hand-off, and a paused member holds its work
pr: 2897
surface: [engine, app, chat]
invalidates:
  - "team_send answered every directive with \"A member that is idle starts a turn on it now, and a busy one reads it at its next step\" whatever happened. It now waits up to four seconds on the team's traffic and says for each member one of: \"@web started a turn on it.\", \"@web is in a turn and reads it at the next step.\", \"@web did NOT start: <why>.\", \"@web is waiting on the person and reads it once they answer.\", or \"@web has shown no sign of starting yet; team_status says whether it did.\" Where a member did not start, the answer also tells the manager not to tell the person the work is under way."
  - "A member's pause lived only in the desktop app's own file (app/crews.json, members.<crew>/<agent>.paused) and the engine did not know of it: a directive opened a paused member, started its turn and logged \"woke @x\", and the app then stopped that turn, so the directive was consumed and Resume replayed nothing. The pause is now the team's record (teams.Member.Paused, File.SetPaused). A member paused in any of its teams is not opened or woken by any team's traffic (File.PausedAnywhere), shows as paused in each of its crews, and is resumed from any of them (SetPaused false clears every membership); the directive stays undelivered and starts the member's turn when the person resumes it, including after a restart. A turn something else starts in a paused member (a command's ending, a task landing) is handed none of its teams' traffic and moves no cursor; a turn the person's own words open still reads what waited, and Resume then starts no turn on lines already read. The app carries an earlier build's pause onto the team's record once."
  - "A directive to a paused member wrote \"woke @x\" then \"stopped\" to the traffic. It now writes one line from system, \"held @x: the person paused it; it starts on this when they resume it\", answering the directive, and Resume writes an event from you with the text \"resumed\" to every open crew the member belongs to, whichever one it was pressed in."
  - "A member's turn that anybody stopped ended with the event text \"stopped\" (state idle), which neither woke the manager nor was delivered to it. A stop by the person of a member that is on its manager's work (a directive, or the brief a started member begins on) now ends \"stopped by the person\" (teams.StoppedByPerson), once per hand-off; it wakes the manager and is delivered as \"@x's turn on what you handed it was stopped by the person before it finished, so that work is not done. The person chose to stop it: tell them where it stands, and do not hand it out again unless they ask.\" The work is under way from the hand-off until a turn finishes, or fails, with no background command left that the member waits on (one whose ending it is owed or asked to be notified of, or a watch that has not fired): a turn such a command's ending starts is still on it, and so is a turn the person opened with their own words while the member waits on one. A stop by the person with none of the manager's work under way, a stop by the manager's team_stop (whichever interface performs it), the desktop app quitting, and the app ending a paused member's stray wake still end \"stopped\" and still wake nobody; the app's two stops now cancel through StopByClosing and StopByWorkStopped, where they used the person's door. The global manager is not woken by either."
  - "team_status and the manager's digest gave a member's state as running, asking, idle or failed only. A member's line may now read \"paused by the person; it starts nothing until they resume it\" or \"on standby; no work handed out yet\", carries the member's role after its handle (\"- @dev, macOS developer\"), and may add \"has not started on #22\" (only for a member that is not in a turn) or \"its last turn was stopped by the person\"."
  - "Accepting a crew proposal (crews.create from proposal) started every helper at once on its brief. Each helper now joins on standby (teams.Member.Standby, cleared by the member's session once its start line is handed to it): its start line starts no turn, however much later the first directive comes, the traffic says \"opened @x on standby; it starts on its manager's first directive\", and the first directive hands it that line marked \"◆ your standing role from manager (what you are here for, not work to begin; work arrives as a directive)\" in place of \"◆ brief from manager\". team_start and members.start still start a member on its brief at once."
  - "A proposal's role name was kept only as an app-layer role with no instructions, so it reached neither the member nor the manager. It is now also the team's record (teams.Member.Role, File.SetRole): the member's team note says \"Your role in this team: <role>.\", and the manager's roster shows the role in place of the title (\"@dev (macOS developer)\"). members.role and a role's rename write it too."
  - "The crew proposal prompt asked for each helper's brief as the \"whole assignment\". It now asks for \"what this helper is responsible for\", and says an accepted crew joins on standby."
  - "The desktop app stopped every turn it had not started itself on a paused member, including one the person opened by typing to that member from another window. It now leaves a turn the person's own words opened alone, and still stops any other wake (a background process ending, a task landing)."
  - "The manager of a crew could be paused from the app like any member. SetPaused now refuses it: \"the manager of <crew> is not paused; stop its turn instead\". It also refuses a member that manages another open team (\"@lead manages <team> and is not paused; stop its turn instead\"), and making a conversation a manager lifts its pause."
  - "After the person stopped a turn, the ending of a background command that turn had left running (and a watch's firing) started the conversation's next turn, in a team as anywhere else: a stopped member whose command later exited was woken and reported the stopped work done. For a conversation with a role in any team, from a stop of a running turn (the person's, or a manager's team_stop, whether an interface performs it or the member's own session does) until a turn next begins, such an ending is written into the conversation as a record with \"This arrived after this conversation's turn was stopped, so it started no turn. It is a record of what happened: do not take the stopped work up again because of it.\" and starts no turn. A stop by the person that finds no turn running but a command or watch still running, or a failed command's ending about to start a turn, begins the same hold; that pending wake is withdrawn and its note is read by the next turn. Only when the conversation is waiting on one of them for work a manager handed it does it also write \"stopped by the person\" to that manager's team, which wakes the manager; a command left running for its own sake, such as a dev server, tells no manager anything. The hold covers a background command's ending and a watch's firing and nothing else: a background task landing, the person's own message and the manager's next hand-off still start the conversation, and that turn ends the hold. The command is still left running by the stop, and a conversation in no team is unchanged."

A manager model reports what its tools tell it. In the measured run a member the
person had paused was handed two directives, the tool said both had started, and
the manager told the person so twice before finding an empty folder. Creating
that crew had also started every helper on its line of the proposal, one of which
began work nobody had asked for.
